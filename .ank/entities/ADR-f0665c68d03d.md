## Why

The reader's entities panel lists every kind side by side: tasks, decisions,
specifications and log entries in one column. Today each identifier is yellow
whatever it names, so the only way to tell a `TASK-` row from an `ADR-` row is
to read its prefix. On a corpus of fifteen hundred entities, where most rows are
tasks, finding the handful of decisions in the list means reading every row.
That is the job colour does well, and the one this palette forbids it.

## Why a decision, and a supersession behind it

SPEC-89070ce7f3b8 is accepted and bounds the palette "here rather than in the
code", so the row saying identifiers are yellow cannot move by a change to
`meaning.rs` alone. The table anticipates this in its own words: a surface
wanting `ADR-` to read differently from `TASK-` has to change the rows of the
table, not add a call site nobody reviews. This decision is that change, made
where it is reviewed.

An ADR cannot amend a specification, so the palette row moves by superseding
the document that carries it. The task implementing this decision proposes that
supersession beside the code, so that no commit on the default branch has the
code and the accepted specification disagreeing about one colour.

ADR-1f70ce2c3eac is untouched. It requires one table and allows one renderer per
surface, and both still hold: what changes is what the table says, not where it
lives or who reads it.

## What the colours are, and why these

Both surfaces paint on the eight colours of the terminal's own palette, and the
status column already spends most of them. Overlap is unavoidable, so the
choice keeps it harmless:

- **task: yellow.** Unchanged. Tasks are most of any corpus, and keeping them
  where they were means most of what a person has already learned does not move.
- **adr: magenta.** The colour of `proposed`, the state a decision most often
  waits in for a human. A decision reading in the colour of "waiting on
  somebody" is a resemblance, not a contradiction.
- **spec: blue.** Rare in a listing, and reference material nobody claims.
- **log: dim.** A log entry records what happened and asks for nothing.

The identifier and the status never share a column, so a magenta `ADR-` and a
magenta `[proposed]` on one row are told apart by where they stand, and the
kind is always spelled in the prefix.

## What it costs

`Role::Identifier` leaves the shared table, so `ank-cli`'s `style.rs` and
`ank-tui`'s `paint.rs` stop compiling until each renders the four new roles.
That is the intended effect of a total match on `Role`. The CLI's listings
(`ank find`, `ank context`, `ank show`) change colour too, and that is
deliberate: one table means the CLI and the reader agree that a decision looks
like a decision.
